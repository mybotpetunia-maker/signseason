December's Rare and Ancient Gems

December babies, you get a birthstone pairing that spans the extremes of gemstone history. Tanzanite was not even discovered until 1967, making it one of the youngest gemstones in the modern catalog. Turquoise has been mined and treasured for over 7,000 years, making it one of the oldest. Together, they represent the full spectrum of what a birthstone can be: cutting-edge rarity and ancient wisdom, all in one month.

Tanzanite exists in only one place on the entire planet: a small mining area near Mount Kilimanjaro in Tanzania. That is it. Nowhere else on Earth has the unique geological conditions needed to create this violet-blue gemstone. Geologists estimate the deposits could be exhausted within 20 to 30 years, which makes tanzanite roughly 1,000 times rarer than diamond. If you own tanzanite, you own something that literally cannot be replaced once the mines run out. The stone displays a phenomenon called pleochroism, showing different colors (blue, violet, and burgundy) depending on the viewing angle. It is mesmerizing.

Turquoise, by contrast, has been a cornerstone of human adornment since the dawn of civilization. Egyptian pharaohs wore it. Persian royalty covered their thrones in it. Native American nations have considered it sacred for millennia. The stone's distinctive blue-green color, often webbed with brown or black matrix, is so iconic that we named a color after it. Turquoise forms through a chemical reaction between copper, aluminum, and phosphorus in arid environments, which is why the finest specimens come from Iran, the American Southwest, and China.

How to Wear and Use December Birthstones

Tanzanite is best worn as earrings or a pendant to keep its energy near the upper chakras. It is softer than sapphire at 6 to 7 on the Mohs scale, so rings should have protective settings and be removed during physical activities. The stone's pleochroism means it looks different from every angle, which makes pendant settings particularly beautiful as the stone catches light throughout the day. Tanzanite should be cleaned gently with warm water and mild soap. Never use ultrasonic or steam cleaners.

Turquoise has been worn as rings, pendants, bracelets, and belt buckles for millennia, and all placements work well. The stone is softer and more porous than many gems, so it should be kept away from chemicals, perfume, and prolonged water exposure. Turquoise can actually change color over time, becoming greener or darker as it absorbs oils and minerals from your skin. Many turquoise lovers consider this a feature, not a bug, as the stone literally transforms with you.

For energetic work, tanzanite on the third eye during meditation facilitates deep visionary states. Turquoise held in the hands or placed on the throat encourages honest, compassionate communication and provides an overall sense of protection and wellbeing. Frequently Asked Questions

What are the December birthstones?

December has two primary birthstones: tanzanite and turquoise. Tanzanite is a violet-blue gemstone found only in Tanzania, while turquoise is an ancient blue-green stone treasured by cultures worldwide for thousands of years.

What zodiac signs connect to December birthstones?

Why is tanzanite so special?

Tanzanite is found in only one place on Earth: a small mining area near Mount Kilimanjaro in Tanzania. Geologists estimate the supply could be exhausted within 20-30 years, making it roughly 1,000 times rarer than diamond.

How can you tell if turquoise is real?

Real turquoise feels cool to the touch, has a waxy luster, and typically shows natural matrix patterns (dark veins). It will not be perfectly uniform in color. Genuine turquoise also will not melt or produce chemical smells when touched with a hot needle, unlike plastic imitations.
