December's Remarkable Birthstone Duo

December babies, your two birthstones could not be more different, and that is exactly what makes them perfect for the month that closes one year and opens the possibility of the next. Turquoise is one of the oldest gemstones known to civilization, worn by pharaohs, Native American chiefs, and Tibetan monks for millennia. Tanzanite was not even discovered until 1967 and comes from a single location on the entire planet. Ancient and modern, earthly and cosmic, grounded and transcendent. December contains multitudes, and so do you.

Turquoise has been treasured for over 7,000 years, making it one of the first gemstones ever mined. That distinctive sky-blue to greenish-blue color, often veined with dark matrix patterns, is instantly recognizable. The finest turquoise historically came from Persia (now Iran), with exceptional material also found in the American Southwest, particularly Arizona and New Mexico. For the Navajo, Zuni, and other indigenous peoples, turquoise is not just decorative. It is sacred, a bridge between earth and sky, the physical world and the spiritual realm.

Tanzanite is a variety of zoisite found only in a small mining area near Mount Kilimanjaro in Tanzania. It displays a mesmerizing violet-blue color that shifts between blue and purple depending on the viewing angle, a phenomenon called pleochroism. Tiffany and Co. introduced tanzanite to the world market in 1968, and it quickly became one of the most popular colored gemstones. Geologists estimate the single known deposit could be exhausted within decades, making tanzanite roughly a thousand times rarer than diamond. If you own one, you hold something the Earth may never produce again.

How to Wear and Use December Birthstones

Turquoise is best worn as a pendant near the throat or as a ring. Silver settings are the traditional choice and many practitioners believe silver enhances turquoise's protective energy. Because turquoise is porous and relatively soft, it requires more care than many gemstones. Avoid contact with water, chemicals, perfume, and sunscreen. Clean with a dry, soft cloth only. Some turquoise will change color over time, becoming greener or darker, which many cultures interpret as the stone absorbing negative energy on your behalf or bonding with its wearer.

For energetic protection, carry turquoise in your pocket or wear it as a bracelet. Place it near your front door to protect your home. In Native American traditions, turquoise given as a gift carries stronger protective energy than turquoise purchased for yourself, which makes it an exceptionally meaningful present for someone you care about.

Tanzanite makes stunning jewelry but is somewhat delicate at 6-7 on the Mohs scale and has one direction of perfect cleavage. Earrings and pendants are safer choices than rings for daily wear. For meditation, hold tanzanite at the third eye (center of forehead) and breathe deeply. Its triple-chakra activation creates a column of light energy from throat to crown that many practitioners describe as one of the most powerful meditation experiences available with any single stone. Frequently Asked Questions

What are the December birthstones?

December has two primary birthstones: tanzanite and turquoise. Tanzanite is a rare violet-blue stone found only in Tanzania, while turquoise is one of the oldest known gemstones with a sky-blue to green color prized across cultures for thousands of years.

What zodiac signs connect to December birthstones?

December's birthstones bridge Sagittarius (Nov 22 - Dec 21) and Capricorn (Dec 22 - Jan 19). Tanzanite supports Sagittarius's spiritual expansion, while turquoise grounds Capricorn's ambition with ancient wisdom and protection.

Why is tanzanite so rare?

Tanzanite is found in only one location on Earth: a small mining area near Mount Kilimanjaro in Tanzania. Geologists estimate the supply could be exhausted within a few decades, making it roughly 1,000 times rarer than diamond.

How do I care for turquoise jewelry?

Turquoise is porous and relatively soft (5-6 on Mohs scale). Avoid water, chemicals, perfume, and prolonged sun exposure. Clean with a dry soft cloth. Store separately from harder stones to prevent scratching. Natural turquoise may change color over time, which many consider a sign of the stone bonding with its wearer.
